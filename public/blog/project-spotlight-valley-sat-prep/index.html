Project Spotlight: Valley SAT Prep

A practice test can tell you that a student missed six questions. That does not tell a teacher what to do on Monday morning.

At Hack the Valley 2026, Jordan Garcia and the Sequoia Sasquatches built Valley SAT Prep to close that gap. Their classroom-focused diagnostic turns a short SAT practice session into specific next steps for both students and educators. The project won Best Education at the event.

From a score to a plan

On the student side, the prototype delivers a mixed reading, writing, and math diagnostic. It tracks answers, confidence, and time, then turns the result into a predicted score, a skill-by-skill gap analysis, and a focused study plan.

The tutor mode is deliberately constrained: it gives hints and strategy during the diagnostic instead of simply handing over the answer. That is a small product decision with a big consequence—the tool can help a student get unstuck without making the assessment meaningless.

The teacher view is the real move

Most test-prep tools stop at the student score. Valley SAT Prep keeps going. Its teacher dashboard rolls results up into class averages, priority skills, common mistake patterns, student groups, and timing or confidence flags. The point is not more analytics. The point is to help an educator decide what to reteach, who needs help, and which practice questions to use next.

  • Class-wide skill breakdowns instead of a pile of disconnected scores.
  • Student grouping around shared gaps so support can be targeted.
  • Timing and confidence signals that show when the problem may be hesitation, not just content knowledge.
  • English and Spanish interface support, plus translation-aware question filtering for image-heavy prompts.

Built for classrooms, not a demo reel

The useful idea underneath Valley SAT Prep is simple: a diagnostic should produce an action, not just a number. Students need to know what to work on. Teachers need to know what the class is struggling with. The project uses one result set to serve both.

That is exactly the kind of work Hack the Valley exists to surface—local builders taking a problem they understand and making a concrete tool around it.
